two-week cycle.

Two caveats on that number, both understating it:
  - Slippage is recorded to the cent. On tight single-leg LOTTO tickets the
    mid and the modeled fill round to the same cent, so ~$0.30/ticket of real
    modeled cost is lost to rounding.
  - It excludes the four still-open tickets' exit costs.

WHAT THE ENGINE GOT RIGHT, AND WHAT BROKE
------------------------------------------
Both closing paths ran live and computed correctly: the 25% profit target
(+$49.84) and the expiry-day sellout (four times). The bounded-loss invariant
held throughout — no naked shorts, book never below zero.

Five defects were found and fixed during the window, three of them the same
species: a silent no-trade that would have looked like "no opportunities."
  1. Delta/strike band was wrong by ~5% of spot. Caught pre-flight against
     live data; would have found zero candidates every single day.
  2. Migrated v1 positions had no exit rules at all (sleeve "legacy" absent
     from the table). Would have drifted past the window unmanaged.
  3. Half-strikes printed as whole ones (232.5 shown as 232 — a different,
     real contract). Reporting only; fixed with a test.
  4. QQQ lists $1 strikes only on the current week's expiries; 09-14, 09-15
     and 09-16 are $5-only, where a $2-wide spread cannot be built at all.
     The runbook's "nearest 5 DTE" rule would have selected an unusable
     expiry and the sleeve would have silently found nothing.
  5. max_loss is a settlement cap, not a sellout cap. FAT-04 stated $131 and
     realized -$137.16, because a force-close pays the spread where
     settlement does not.

STRUCTURAL FINDING: THE SLEEVES DO NOT FIT THE BANKROLL
-------------------------------------------------------
FAT tickets cost $131-150 of risk each. LOTTO tickets are capped at $67.50.
With $500 total and 100% deployable, three FAT positions plus the two legacy
spreads saturate the book. The consequences showed up repeatedly:
  - On 4 of 7 v2 sessions, a sleeve produced a valid candidate and the
    capital-at-risk ceiling refused it — including 09-04, when FAT found its
    cleanest entry of the week (0.345 delta against a 0.35 target).
  - LOTTO opened just 2 tickets in the whole window. On every other day its
    candidates cleared the delta band and failed on ticket size, not signal.
    PLTR, TSLA, AMD and COIN were never affordable at 0.15-0.35 delta.

Both sleeves were pre-registered to deploy ~98% of the book together. In
practice they crowded each other out. That is a sizing conclusion the paper
window was built to surface, and it did.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT PROVE
----------------------------------
It does NOT prove the strategy is bad. Five closed trades is statistically
nothing; a -25% fortnight on a maximum-risk mandate is well inside the range
of outcomes for a book with no stops that rides defined-risk spreads into
expiry. One QQQ spread getting run over on 09-03 accounts for essentially the
entire loss.

It DOES establish, with real venue data:
  - the cost model, measured rather than assumed: 4.8% of bankroll, 5.3% of
    premium, per two-week cycle;
  - that the settlement and force-close machinery is correct;
  - that the ops loop is reliable enough to run unattended;
  - that the sleeve sizing is wrong for $500.

And the published evidence base has not changed: every structure in this
desk has documented negative expected value (JoF 2023 on retail option
buying; Review of Finance 2025 on earnings buys; Carr & Wu on single-name
variance premium; WPUT vs PUT on cadence). The window measured mechanics and
costs, not edge, because two weeks cannot measure edge.

THE DECISION
------------
Three options, with my honest read on each.

1. FUND A REAL DESK.  Requires options Level 3 approval on the account plus
   $1,000-2,500 of dedicated capital — the current $261 balance cannot carry
   a single FAT ticket, let alone a sleeve. I do not recommend this. Nothing
   in this window produced evidence of edge, the sizing is demonstrably
   wrong, and the go/no-go gate in FIRM.md (a full gauntlet against
   historical chains) has never been run for options.

2. EXTEND PAPER, RESIZED.  The cheapest way to learn more. Concretely: drop
   to one sleeve at a time so they stop crowding each other, or raise the
   simulated bankroll to $2,000 so both sleeves can size properly and the
   $67.50 LOTTO cap stops being the binding constraint. Also worth running
   the FIRM.md gauntlet against historical chains before any more forward
   testing — the quote snapshots archived in state/options_quotes/ are the
   start of that dataset.

3. STOP.  Defensible. The desk works; the evidence says the trades do not.

My recommendation is 2, and specifically: fix the bankroll-to-ticket ratio
before collecting another day of data, because 4 of the last 7 sessions the
engine was making sizing decisions rather than trading decisions.
